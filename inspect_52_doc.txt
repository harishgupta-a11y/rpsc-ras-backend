
Q. Which of the following constitutional provisions specifically addresses the citizenship status of a person who migrated to Pakistan after March 1, 1947, but subsequently returned to India under a permit for resettlement or permanent return?

A) Article 7 of the Constitution of India

B) Article 6 of the Constitution of India

C) Article 8 of the Constitution of India

D) Article 9 of the Constitution of India

Correct: A

Explanation: Article 7 of the Constitution of India deals with the citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan. It states that a person who migrated from the territory of India to the territory now included in Pakistan after March 1, 1947, shall not be deemed to be a citizen of India. However, an exception is made for those who returned to India under a permit for resettlement or permanent return issued by or under the authority of any law, making them eligible to be registered as citizens under conditions similar to those under Article 6.

Q. Under the Citizenship Act of 1955, a person born outside India on or after December 3, 2004, can acquire Indian citizenship by descent only if which of the following conditions is fulfilled?

A) Either of the parents is a citizen of India at the time of birth, and the birth is registered at an Indian consulate within five years.

B) Both parents are citizens of India, or one parent is a citizen and the other is not an illegal migrant at the time of birth.

C) The birth is registered at an Indian consulate within one year of its occurrence, or with the permission of the Central Government after that period, along with an explicit declaration that the minor does not hold the passport of another country.

D) The person resides ordinarily in India for a continuous period of seven years before making an application to the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Correct: C

Explanation: According to the amendments made to the Citizenship Act of 1955, a person born outside India on or after December 3, 2004, shall not be a citizen of India by descent unless their birth is registered at an Indian consulate within one year of the date of birth, or with the permission of the Central Government after the expiry of the said period. The application for registration must be accompanied by an undertaking in writing from the parents of such minor child that he or she does not hold the passport of another country.

Q. Indian citizenship can be terminated through three distinct modalities under the Citizenship Act of 1955. Which of the following correctly identifies these three methods?

A) Renunciation, Registration, and Naturalisation

B) Deprivation, Termination, and Descent

C) Renunciation, Termination, and Deprivation

D) Abdication, Cancellation, and Deletion

Correct: C

Explanation: The Citizenship Act of 1955 specifies three ways of losing citizenship: Renunciation, which is a voluntary declaration by an Indian citizen of full age and capacity; Termination, which happens automatically by operation of law when an Indian citizen voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country; and Deprivation, which is a compulsory termination of Indian citizenship by the Central Government on grounds such as obtaining citizenship by fraud, showing disloyalty to the Constitution, or unlawfully trading with the enemy during war.
