
Q. Which part of the Indian Constitution contains provisions related to Citizenship?

A) Part I

B) Part II

C) Part III

D) Part IV

Correct: B

Explanation: Part II of the Indian Constitution covers citizenship. It spans from Article 5 to Article 11.

Q. Which Article defines citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution?

A) Article 6

B) Article 5

C) Article 7

D) Article 8

Correct: B

Explanation: Article 5 deals with citizenship at the commencement. It covers individuals domiciled in India on January 26, 1950.

Q. Which Article regulates the citizenship rights of migrants from Pakistan to India?

A) Article 6

B) Article 7

C) Article 8

D) Article 9

Correct: A

Explanation: Article 6 governs citizenship rights for Pakistani migrants. It establishes specific cut-off dates for such migration.

Q. Which Article deals with the citizenship of persons who migrated to Pakistan after March 1, 1947?

A) Article 5

B) Article 6

C) Article 7

D) Article 8

Correct: C

Explanation: Article 7 handles individuals migrating to Pakistan after March 1, 1947. It provides exceptions for those returning with resettlement permits.

Q. Which Article addresses the citizenship of persons of Indian origin residing outside India?

A) Article 5

B) Article 7

C) Article 8

D) Article 9

Correct: C

Explanation: Article 8 covers Indian origin persons residing abroad. They must register via diplomatic channels to claim citizenship.

Q. According to Article 9, a person loses Indian citizenship upon voluntarily acquiring nationality of which state?

A) Any foreign state

B) Only Commonwealth nations

C) Only neighboring nations

D) Any enemy state

Correct: A

Explanation: Article 9 bars holding citizenship of any foreign state. India explicitly follows a single citizenship framework.

Q. Which Article guarantees the continuance of citizenship rights subject to parliamentary laws?

A) Article 8

B) Article 9

C) Article 10

D) Article 11

Correct: C

Explanation: Article 10 ensures continuance of citizenship rights. These rights remain subject to any future parliamentary legislation.

Q. Which constitutional provision empowers Parliament to completely regulate the right of citizenship by law?

A) Article 8

B) Article 9

C) Article 10

D) Article 11

Correct: D

Explanation: Article 11 empowers Parliament to pass comprehensive legislation. This authority led to the Citizenship Act of 1955.

Q. In which year did the Parliament pass the principal Citizenship Act?

A) 1950

B) 1952

C) 1955

D) 1956

Correct: C

Explanation: The principal Citizenship Act was enacted in 1955. It provides detailed pathways for acquiring and losing nationality.

Q. How many modes of acquiring Indian citizenship are specified under the Citizenship Act, 1955?

A) Three

B) Four

/C) Five

D) Six

Correct: C

Explanation: Five distinct modes exist under the 1955 Act. These are birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, and incorporation.

Q. Which type of citizenship model does the Indian Constitution establish?

A) Dual citizenship

B) Single citizenship

C) Regional citizenship

D) Federal citizenship

Correct: B

Explanation: India features a strict single citizenship model. This concept was adapted directly from the United Kingdom.

Q. Which Ministry processes regular applications for the registration or naturalisation of Indian citizenship?

A) Ministry of External Affairs

B) Ministry of Home Affairs

C) Ministry of Law and Justice

D) Ministry of Defence

Correct: B

Explanation: The Ministry of Home Affairs handles citizenship matters. It acts as the nodal administrative body for processing applications.

Q. A person born in India on or after January 26, 1950, but before July 1, 1987, is a citizen by which mode?

A) By Descent

B) By Registration

C) By Birth

D) By Naturalisation

Correct: C

Explanation: This represents citizenship by birth under jus soli rules. Birth inside the territory automatically conferred citizenship during this period.

Q. Citizenship by descent for a person born outside India before 1992 required the father to hold what status?

A) Indian citizen

B) Permanent resident

C) Government employee

D) OCI cardholder

Correct: A

Explanation: The father had to be an Indian citizen. The 1992 amendment later extended this right to mothers.

Q. Which statutory schedule lists the qualifications needed to obtain Indian citizenship through naturalisation?

A) Second Schedule

B) Third Schedule

C) Eighth Schedule

D) Ninth Schedule

Correct: B

Explanation: The Third Schedule details all qualifications for naturalisation. It specifies residency and language criteria for foreign applicants.

Q. How many languages are currently recognized in the Eighth Schedule, one of which a naturalised citizen must know?

A) 14

B) 18

C) 22

D) 25

Correct: C

Explanation: The Eighth Schedule recognizes exactly 22 regional languages. An applicant for naturalisation must have adequate knowledge of one.

Q. What is the historical cut-off date established under Article 6 for migrants coming from Pakistan?

A) June 3, 1947

B) August 15, 1947

C) July 19, 1948

D) January 26, 1950

Correct: C

Explanation: July 19, 1948, serves as the migration cut-off. This date matches the introduction of the permit system.

Q. How many methods of losing Indian citizenship are recognized under the Citizenship Act, 1955?

A) Two

B) Three

C) Four

D) Five

Correct: B

Explanation: The Act outlines three methods of losing citizenship. These methods are renunciation, termination, and compulsory deprivation.

Q. Automatic termination of Indian citizenship happens when a person takes up citizenship of which entity?

A) A foreign country

B) A public corporation

C) A local state

D) An international agency

Correct: A

Explanation: Citizenship terminates upon acquiring foreign nationality. This follows the constitutional mandate against dual citizenship.

Q. Compulsory deprivation of citizenship by the Central Government applies to which category of citizens?

A) Citizens by birth

B) Registered or naturalised citizens

C) Citizens by descent only

D) All categories uniformly

Correct: B

Explanation: Deprivation applies specifically to registered or naturalised individuals. It triggers upon fraud, disloyalty, or criminal conviction.

Q. Under the Citizenship Act, an Indian citizen of full age can voluntarily execute which document?

A) Certificate of Naturalisation

B) Declaration of Renunciation

/C) Instrument of Accession

D) Oath of Allegiance

Correct: B

Explanation: An adult citizen can execute a renunciation declaration. This voluntarily strips them of their Indian nationality status.

Q. Entry 17 of which legislative list leaves the subject of citizenship exclusively with the Union Parliament?

A) Union List

B) State List

C) Concurrent List

D) Residury List

Correct: A

Explanation: Entry 17 of the Union List covers citizenship. Consequently, state legislatures have no power to pass citizenship laws.

Q. In which landmark case did the Supreme Court confirm that corporations are not citizens under Article 19?

A) Berubari Case

B) State Trading Corporation of India v. CTO

C) Kesavananda Bharati Case

D) Maneka Gandhi Case

Correct: B

Explanation: The State Trading Corporation case drew this distinction. It clarified that companies hold nationality but lack political citizenship.

Q. Which category of cards did the Government of India completely merge into the OCI card scheme in 2015?

A) NRI Card

/B) PIO Card

C) Aadhaar Card

D) National Identity Card

Correct: B

Explanation: Person of Indian Origin (PIO) cards were merged. They are now statutorily treated as OCI cards.

Q. Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholders are strictly prohibited from purchasing which type of asset in India?

A) Industrial machinery

B) Residential apartments

C) Agricultural land

D) Commercial buildings

Correct: C

Explanation: OCI cardholders cannot buy agricultural or plantation lands. They are only permitted to purchase non-agricultural immovable property.

Q. Do OCI cardholders possess the fundamental right to vote in general elections in India?

A) Yes, automatically

B) No, they do not

C) Only in state elections

D) Only via postal ballots

Correct: B

Explanation: OCI cardholders have no voting rights in India. They are also barred from holding any public employment.
